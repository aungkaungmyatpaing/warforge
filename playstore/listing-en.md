## Full description (4000 characters max)

Take a hundred years of military engineering apart and put it back together.

Warforge gives you twenty-one real vehicles, from the Mark IV that crawled across the Somme in 1917 to the nuclear carrier USS Nimitz. Each one is assembled piece by piece — engine first, then the running gear, the fighting compartment, the armour — and each one can then be opened up and looked inside.

ASSEMBLE
Drag each part onto the blueprint in the right order. Six stages per vehicle, from the powerpack to the last thing bolted on the turret. Decoy parts from other vehicles are mixed into the tray, so you have to know what you are looking at.

LOOK INSIDE
Every vehicle has a full X-ray layer: the engine, the gearbox, each crew position, the ammunition, the fuel and the armour, all colour-coded and all where they really were. Slide the cutaway open and see a Panzer IV's driveshaft running the length of the hull — and then see why a T-34 with its gearbox at the back sits thirty centimetres lower on the same suspension.

READ THE HISTORY
Every vehicle comes with the story of why it is shaped the way it is. Not specifications — arguments. Why the Mark IV is a rhomboid. Why the Spitfire's elliptical wing was worth the misery of building it. Why Yamato's guns were obsolete before she fired them in anger.

TEST YOURSELF
A short quiz on each vehicle, drawn from what the cutaway actually shows.

TWENTY-ONE VEHICLES
• Ground — Mark IV, Renault FT, Vickers Medium Mk II, Panzer IV Ausf. H, T-34-85, Centurion, M1A2 Abrams
• Air — Sopwith Camel, Fokker Dr.I, Ju 52/3m, Supermarine Spitfire Mk V, B-17G Flying Fortress, MiG-15, F-16C
• Naval — HMS Dreadnought, SM U-9, Admiral Graf Spee, Fletcher-class destroyer, Yamato, Los Angeles-class submarine, USS Nimitz

Every model is built to real dimensions, in 3D, with the camouflage and markings they actually wore.
